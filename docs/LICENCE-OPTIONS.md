# TIMET — LICENCE OPTIONS FOR THE OPEN SOUND DATABASE

Status: DRAFT. Not legal advice. Have counsel review before publishing,
especially the AI and text-and-data-mining clauses.

Decision taken by Lorenzo Brusci: PROTECTIVE BUT EXPLICIT.
Meaning: the database is open to listening, study, and non-commercial creative reuse,
but commercial exploitation and commercial AI training are explicitly reserved.
The reservation must be stated in plain language AND in machine-readable form.

---

## Option A — CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (off the shelf)

Grants: share and adapt, attribution required, non-commercial only, derivatives under
the same licence.

Pros: recognised, understood by platforms, no drafting needed.
Cons: silent about AI training. Data-mining companies routinely treat "non-commercial"
as ambiguous, and CC has no effective machine-readable TDM reservation. Weakest link
for the exact problem you are trying to solve.

Verdict: usable as a fallback, insufficient as a statement.

---

## Option B — Timet Open Sound Object Licence v1.0 (TOSOL) — RECOMMENDED

A short custom licence, drafted to say one clear thing well.

### Granted, without fee
- Listening, private and public, non-commercial
- Study, teaching, and academic research, including computational analysis by
  research organisations within the meaning of Art. 2(1) of Directive (EU) 2019/790
- Creative reuse in non-commercial works: composition, performance, installation,
  film, radio, dance, theatre, with attribution
- Redistribution of the unmodified objects, non-commercial, with this licence attached
- Citation and quotation in criticism, journalism, and scholarship

### Reserved
- Commercial use of any kind, including commercial release of works incorporating
  the objects, advertising, and commercial installations
- Text and data mining for commercial purposes
- Use of the objects, or of any dataset derived from them, as training data for
  machine learning or generative AI systems of any kind, commercial or otherwise,
  without a separate written licence
- Re-licensing, sub-licensing, or distribution of the objects under different terms
- Use of the Timet name and marks beyond factual attribution

### Reservation of rights, machine-readable
This licence constitutes an express reservation of rights in a machine-readable form
pursuant to Article 4(3) of Directive (EU) 2019/790. The reservation is expressed in:
- `dataset.json` and `dataset.csv`, per-object field `tdm_reservation: "reserved"`
- `dataset.json` root field `licence: "TOSOL-1.0"` and `tdm_policy: "reserved"`
- HTTP response headers on all dataset endpoints
- `robots.txt` directives for automated agents
- A human-readable statement at `/open-database/licence`

### Attribution format
"Sound object: [title], Timet, [year]. Used under the Timet Open Sound Object
Licence v1.0. timet.org/open-database/licence"

### Conditions
- Attribution is required in a reasonably visible place, not buried
- Modified objects must be identified as modified
- Combinations generated by visitors in the recombination engine are the visitor's
  work for non-commercial purposes; commercial use still requires a licence
- No warranty; objects are provided as found in the archive

### Enforcement posture
State plainly, on the licence page, that the reservation is deliberate, that Timet
licenses commercial and AI uses on request, and that requests are welcome.
An invitation to negotiate is worth more than a threat.

## Provenance note

Several hundred source files exist. Masters remain in the Pro Tools and Kyma sessions,
recoverable on request but not published. The public database publishes derived,
normalised objects with documented provenance, plus metadata-only entries for material
that exists but is not released. Being explicit about what is NOT available is part of
the credibility of the claim.

## Text to publish on /open-database/licence

Suggested opening, plain language, no legalese in the first paragraph:

"Timet has published individual sound objects since 1993, when we argued that a
composition could be distributed as its parts as well as its whole. This database
continues that practice.

You may listen, study, teach, and build non-commercial work with these objects.
You may not use them commercially, and you may not use them to train artificial
intelligence systems, without asking us first.

That reservation is deliberate and it is machine-readable. If you want a commercial
or research licence, write to info@musi-co.com. We say yes more often than no."
